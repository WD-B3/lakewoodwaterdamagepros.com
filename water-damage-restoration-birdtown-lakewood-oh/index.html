Birdtown sits in the southeastern corner of Lakewood, next to Madison Park and the Cleveland line. It is a compact residential district of eight streets named for birds, among them Robin, Plover, Lark and Thrush. It was listed as a National Register Historic District in 2006. If water is spreading through a Birdtown home, call (216) 541-8541 and describe what you see.

This page covers what water damage usually involves in a neighborhood of older, closely built houses, and what to do in the first hour. It does not claim that any particular street has flooded or that any house has a particular problem.

About the neighborhood

According to the Lakewood Historical Society, a subsidiary of National Carbon laid out more than 400 lots here in 1894 for workers at a nearby factory. Over the following decades the area filled with small houses, shops and churches. Madison Avenue is the main commercial street, and Berea Road and Plover Street are other streets listed as running through the area.

Survey estimates reported by a public statistics site that repackages Census data suggest that about two thirds of housing units here were built before 1940, that more than half are detached houses and that roughly a quarter are two-unit buildings. Those figures are estimates for the neighborhood as a whole and say nothing about any one address.

What the flood map shows for Birdtown

FEMA's effective flood map places every point we sampled across Birdtown in Zone X, which FEMA describes as an area of minimal flood hazard. Zone X is a map classification. It does not mean water cannot enter a building, because plumbing failures, roof leaks and sewer backups are not flood-map events.

Property-level flood status is not verified here. A lender, insurer or the city can tell you the status of a specific address.

Source type: FEMA National Flood Hazard Layer, effective map data queried in November 2026 at sample points across the area. This is area-level screening and not a property-level determination.

Water problems that fit a Birdtown house

Many Birdtown buildings are two-family houses, and in a two-family house a leak in one unit can reach the other. A burst supply line upstairs can come through the ceiling below within minutes. If you rent one side, tell the owner and the neighbor right away and photograph the damage before anything is moved.

Older houses also tend to have older supply lines, drains and water heaters, but age alone does not tell you whether a part is about to fail. What matters is what you can see: stains, damp walls, a musty smell or a water bill that has jumped. The burst pipe page and the appliance leak page cover the two most common sudden causes.

Heavy rain and the city sewer system

The City of Lakewood says that some of its sewers, built in the early 1900s, use a single pipe for wastewater and stormwater, and that heavy rain, snowmelt and runoff can push excess flow into Lake Erie or the Rocky River. The city has been working on separation and lining projects.

On the evening of August 23, 2023, the National Weather Service reported about 3.5 inches of rain in a couple of hours in Lakewood, with police and fire crews reporting flooded basements at multiple homes across the city. That was a citywide event and the report does not single out Birdtown.

In August 2022, news reports described flooding under the West 117th Street bridge between Berea and Madison avenues, which was a road flooding incident and not a report of basement damage.

Stop the water

Close the main valve or the valve at the fixture, but only if you can reach it without standing in water.

Think about electricity

Do not touch outlets, cords or breakers while standing in water. Call the utility or 911 about sparks or a sagging, wet ceiling.

Keep clear of dirty water

Water from a drain, toilet or sewer can carry contamination. Keep people and pets out and do not use a household vacuum on it.

Photograph first

Take photos and a short video of the damage and the source before moving belongings, when it is safe to do so.

What the work looks like

Crews remove standing water with pumps and extractors, then measure how wet the walls, floors and framing are with moisture meters. Air movers and dehumidifiers are placed to dry the structure, and readings are taken again until the materials reach a dry reading. The water extraction page and the structural drying page explain both steps.

Contaminated water is handled differently. Sewage and drain backups call for protective gear, removal of porous materials and cleaning before drying begins, as described on the sewage backup page. Household fans and a wet vac are not enough for that kind of water.

Insurance paperwork

Whether a loss is covered depends on the cause and on your policy, and your insurer decides. Photos, moisture readings and a written scope of work can help describe what happened and what was done. Ask your insurer what documents it needs and what its deadline is. We do not decide coverage and cannot promise that a claim will be paid.

Water damage questions in Birdtown

Is Birdtown in a FEMA flood zone?

FEMA's effective map shows the points we sampled across Birdtown in Zone X, minimal flood hazard. Status for a specific property is not verified here, and Zone X does not rule out water damage from plumbing, roofs or sewers.

Does a leak in a two-family house affect the other unit?

It can. Water travels through floors, walls and ceilings, so tell the owner and the neighbor and photograph the damage.

Should I clean up before taking photos?

Stop the source and make the area safe first, then photograph the damage before moving items, if it is safe to do so.

Can I dry a wet wall with fans?

Surface fans do not reach water inside walls or under floors. A moisture meter shows what is still wet, and drying equipment is placed to match.

Who decides whether insurance pays?

Your insurer decides based on the cause of the damage and your policy. We can provide photos, readings and a scope of work.
