Keep, Clean or Throw Away After Water Damage in a Lakewood, OH Home

By the 24/7 Lakewood Water Damage Restoration team · Homeowner guide · 2026

☎ Call (216) 541-8541

After a water loss, you face a pile of wet belongings and a hard question about each one. Some things can be dried or cleaned and returned to use. Others should go, either because the water was contaminated or because the material cannot be dried in time. This guide gives a simple way to decide, built around how contaminated the water was and what the item is made of.

Photograph things before discarding them, so your insurer can see what was lost.

Start with the water

The category of water decides most of the answer. Clean water from a supply line gives you the most chance to save items. Water from a washing machine or dishwasher is more contaminated. Sewage and flood water are grossly contaminated, and porous items touched by them are generally discarded. The sewage backup cleanup page covers the worst case.

A starting guide, not a rule for every item

Treat this as a starting point. Value, sentimental weight and the exact conditions can change the call.

Food and drink

Discard food that touched flood or sewage water, including items in packages with screw caps, snap lids or cardboard. Cans that are undamaged can be cleaned and sanitized before opening. When in doubt, throw it out. The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency and public health sources have specific guidance on food and water after flooding, and your local health department can answer questions.

Irreplaceable items

  • Photos and documents: freeze them in sealed bags to stop damage and ask a professional about drying.
  • Art and heirlooms: keep them out of direct heat and call a conservator if they are valuable.
  • Electronics: leave unplugged and have a technician check them before use.
  • Medications: discard those that touched contaminated water, and ask a pharmacist.

Document before you discard

  1. Photograph each item and the damage.
  2. Make a list with brand, age and value.
  3. Keep a sample of carpet or flooring if the insurer needs it.
  4. Bag contaminated items so they do not spread germs.
  5. Ask your adjuster whether they need to see anything first.

A restoration crew can pack out salvageable contents, clean them off site and store them while the structure dries. That is often cheaper than replacing what could have been saved. Ask whether the company offers it, and how it is documented.

Cleaning what you keep

Items that you keep need to be cleaned and dried the right way. Wash hard goods with detergent and warm water, rinse and dry thoroughly. Wash fabrics on the hottest setting that is safe for them. Let wood dry slowly and away from direct heat to avoid cracking.

Do not use a household vacuum on wet debris, and do not turn on electronics that got wet until a technician has checked them. Dry, clean and inspect each item before returning it to use.

When pack-out makes sense

If a large amount of furniture and belongings is affected, a pack-out can be worth considering. The crew inventories items, moves them off site for cleaning and storage, and returns them once the structure is dry and repaired. It also gives crews room to work.

Sentimental items

Some things matter more than their price. Wedding albums, children's drawings and family letters are worth the effort of careful drying even if the odds are uncertain. Separate wet pages with paper, keep them out of direct sun and avoid peeling stuck sheets apart. Freezing buys time while you decide, and a conservator can advise on valuable pieces.

Lakewood note: basements, storage and rentals

Many Lakewood homes have full basements used for storage, and cardboard boxes on the floor soak up water fast. Plastic bins on shelves fare better. After a basement flood, check for the water line on boxes and discard what has sat in it for more than a day.

Census survey figures show that more than half of Lakewood households rent. If you rent, your belongings are covered by your own renter's policy and not the landlord's, so keep your inventory and photos.

Questions homeowners ask

Can I keep a mattress that got wet?

With clean water and quick drying, sometimes, but a mattress touched by contaminated water should be discarded.

Should I throw away wet drywall?

Wet drywall that cannot be dried quickly, or that touched contaminated water, is usually removed.

What do I do with wet photos?

Freeze them in sealed bags to stop damage, then ask a professional about drying.

Do I need to keep damaged items for the adjuster?

Ask your adjuster. Photograph and list items before discarding, and keep samples if asked.

Can wet cardboard boxes in my basement be saved?

Usually not. Cardboard holds water and grows mold fast. Move the contents to plastic bins after checking them.
