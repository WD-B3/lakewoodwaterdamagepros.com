When water is spreading in Cleveland's West Side

Water that is moving across a floor, dripping through a ceiling or rising in a basement is an emergency, whatever hour it is. The 24/7 line is answered at night and on weekends, and emergency dispatch is available around the clock when crews are free. Same-day response is offered when crews are available, and it helps if you can tell us the neighborhood, the floor affected and where the water seems to start.

Stop the source

Close the main water valve or the valve at the fixture. If the water comes from a storm or a drain, stay out of it.

Power and safety

Switch off power to wet rooms only from a dry spot. Call the utility or 911 about sparks, buzzing or a gas smell before calling us.

Lift and photograph

Raise valuables off the floor and photograph the damage before moving things, for your insurer.

Keep clear of dirty water

Keep children and pets out of any water from a drain, toilet or flooding, and wait for the crew.

Neighborhood by neighborhood

ZIP 44102, our primary West Side ZIP, covers Detroit-Shoreway, Cudell, West Boulevard and Edgewater, the neighborhoods that touch Lakewood's eastern border. Detroit-Shoreway is home to the Gordon Square Arts District with the Capitol Theatre, Near West Theatre and Cleveland Public Theatre. A theater, a restaurant or a shop in that district that loses water to a burst line needs a different plan than a house, and the commercial water damage page explains how.

Ohio City and Tremont, in 44113, sit closer to the river. Ohio City has the highest concentration of breweries in the city and hosts the West Side Market, so food and drink businesses with floor drains and walk-in coolers are part of the picture. Old Brooklyn and Brooklyn Centre in 44109, and West Park in 44111, are mostly residential, and West Park is the name locals give to Kamm's Corners, Jefferson, Bellaire-Puritas and Hopkins together.

What we are usually asked to handle here

Homes and businesses on this side of the city report the same mix of problems as the rest of northeast Ohio: leaks from supply lines and appliances, water through a roof or wall after a storm, and flooding in basements. Because many of the buildings are older, water often travels through plaster walls and under layered floors before anyone sees it.

  • Heavy rain and basement flooding, covered under flood damage cleanup.
  • Standing water on a floor from a pipe or appliance, removed through water extraction.
  • Wet floors and walls after the water is gone, dried through structural drying.
  • Water from a drain or toilet, which is treated as contaminated under sewage backup cleanup.

Working across the Lakewood line

The boundary between Lakewood and Cleveland is a city line, not a service line. Cleveland's water comes from the City of Cleveland, and Lakewood buys its drinking water from Cleveland as well, but the sewer and permit authorities differ. If your repair involves permits or a public sewer main, ask the city building department and the city utility for the rules that apply to your address. Rules for permits vary by place, so we do not quote them from memory.

A shared water supply does not mean a shared problem. If the water is coming from the street side, the city utility handles it. If it is on your side of the property, your plumber does, and we handle the removal and drying that follow.

Winter and the West Side

Cleveland Hopkins, on the far West Side, has reached 100 inches of snow in a season only three times since records began in 1893, which tells you that big snow years are the exception and that ordinary cold is the rule. Freezing pipes are a seasonal risk, and our frozen pipe page covers the steps. Check your water shut-off location before the first cold snap.
