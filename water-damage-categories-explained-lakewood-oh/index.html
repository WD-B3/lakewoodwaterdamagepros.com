Water Damage Categories 1, 2 and 3 Explained for Lakewood, OH

By the Lakewood Water Damage Pros team · Homeowner guide · 2026

☎ Call (216) 541-8541

Restoration professionals sort water into three categories, and the category decides almost everything about the job. It tells the crew what protective gear to wear, which materials can be dried and which must be thrown away, and how much cleaning is needed. The categories come from the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration. This guide explains them in plain words for Lakewood homeowners.

The three categories

Water categories at a glance
CategoryPlain descriptionTypical sourcesUsual outcome
Category 1Clean water from a sanitary sourceBroken supply line, tap left running, water heater leakOften dried in place if caught within a day or so
Category 2Water with significant contamination, sometimes called gray waterWashing machine or dishwasher discharge, toilet overflow with urine, sump failureCleaning plus removal of porous materials
Category 3Grossly contaminated water, sometimes called black waterSewage, rising flood water, standing water that has satPorous materials removed, hard surfaces cleaned and disinfected

The categories describe the water at its source and at the moment of contact. They are not the same as how dirty something looks.

Water gets worse with time

Category 1 water does not stay clean. A supply line leak that sat for two or three days in a damp basement can pick up bacteria from surfaces and soil, and a crew may reclassify it. Temperature and the materials it soaked into matter too. That is one reason fast action pays off.

What each category means for your belongings

  • Category 1: carpet, pad, drywall and many contents can often be dried if the response is quick.
  • Category 2: carpet is usually cleaned or removed, pad is generally replaced and some contents are salvageable.
  • Category 3: carpet, pad, insulation and low drywall are removed, and contents that soaked it up are usually discarded.

Hard materials such as concrete, tile and sealed wood are more forgiving in every category. They can usually be cleaned and dried.

Safety and your role

Stay out of Category 3 water if you can, and wear boots and gloves if you cannot. Keep children and pets away, and keep your hands away from your face. Do not use a household vacuum on it. Health questions belong with a physician, and the sewage page covers the work involved.

What a crew does differently by category

How the work changes
StepCategory 1Category 3
Protective gearGloves and bootsFull protective clothing, respirators and gloves
RemovalStanding water extracted, wet materials assessedPorous materials removed and bagged
CleaningSurfaces dried and cleanedSurfaces cleaned and disinfected in stages
DryingAir movers and dehumidifiersSame, after cleaning and removal
DocumentationPhotos and moisture readingsPhotos, readings and disposal records

Notice that drying is part of every category. What changes is what has to happen before drying can begin. In a Category 3 job, the contaminated materials have to be dealt with first, or drying simply moves contamination around.

Questions to ask the crew

  • What category do you think this is, and what did you see that told you?
  • Could the category change if the water stays longer?
  • Which materials will be removed because of the category?
  • How will you protect the rest of the house during cleaning?

A note on terms you may hear

You may hear a crew talk about classes of water damage as well. Classes describe how much water was absorbed and how fast it will evaporate, from Class 1 with little wet material to Class 4 with deep, dense materials such as hardwood or plaster. Category describes the contamination and class describes the amount. Both come from the S500 standard, and together they shape the drying plan.

Lakewood note: why sewage matters here

The City of Lakewood owns and operates its wastewater collection system, with about 166 miles of storm and sanitary sewer mains. The city says groundwater and stormwater entering aging pipes can use up capacity and cause the sanitary system to overload and flood basements.

That makes Category 3 backups a real possibility in heavy rain, and it is the reason to check whether your policy includes sewer backup coverage.

Source type: City of Lakewood sewer information pages (lakewoodoh.gov), a municipal website.

How the category affects cost

A Category 1 job often needs equipment and time but fewer removals. A Category 3 job adds protective gear, disposal and more demolition, so the same square footage costs more. A crew should tell you which category applies and why before work starts.

The two extremes are covered on water extraction for clean water and sewage backup cleanup for Category 3. If you are in Cleveland or Bay Village, the categories work the same way.

Questions homeowners ask

Can Category 1 water become Category 3?

Yes. Time, temperature and what the water touches can raise the category, which is why quick drying matters.

Is rainwater clean?

Not once it enters a building. Rain that comes through a roof or wall picks up contaminants from materials, and flood water from the ground is generally Category 3.

Who decides the category?

The restoration crew assesses it from the source, the conditions and how long the water was present. Your insurer may have its own view.

What category is a toilet overflow?

It depends on what is in the toilet. Clear water from the tank may be Category 1 or 2, while an overflow with waste is Category 3.

Does the city's sewer system make Category 3 more likely in Lakewood?

Heavy rain can overload sewers in many older cities, and the City of Lakewood says this can cause basement flooding. Check your coverage for backups.
