Seasons on the lakeshore

Bay Village covers about seven square miles, and roughly a third of that is lake. A city that borders Lake Erie sees water damage differently through the year, so this page follows the calendar instead of a list of services. Each season has a typical way that water gets into a lakeshore house.

Winter

Cold snaps freeze pipes in exterior walls and unheated porches, and a split often shows up only at the thaw. The steps are covered on the frozen pipe water damage page. Vacation homes and seasonal rentals are the usual victims, because no one is home to notice.

Spring and summer

Heavy rain and wind-driven storms off the lake push water at walls, windows and roofs, and cellars take on water through window wells and foundation walls. Storm damage is on the storm water damage page, and basements on basement water damage cleanup. Humid air and wet materials are also the start of mold, which is why drying should start fast.

Fall

Leaves clog gutters and downspouts, and that sends roof water down the foundation instead of away from it. A walk around the house before the first big rain, with downspouts discharging well away from the walls, is the cheapest fix there is.

When water is spreading in Bay Village

Water moving through a ceiling, a wall or a floor is an emergency at any hour, and emergency dispatch is available around the clock for Bay Village. Same-day response is offered when crews are available. The cards below are the quick version of what to do first.

Close the valve

Shut the main water valve, or the valve at the fixture, as soon as you find the source.

Mind the electrical panel

Cut power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel dry. Otherwise call the utility or 911 first.

Protect what you can

Lift valuables from the floor and photograph damage before cleanup for your insurer.

Stay out of storm water

Water that came in with a storm or up a drain can carry contamination, so keep children and pets away.

What the housing looks like and why it matters

The 2020 census counted 6,466 housing units, and 91.8 percent of occupied units were owner-occupied. That means most of the decisions about a loss, from the plumber to the insurer to the restoration crew, are the homeowner's to make and coordinate. The streets are tree-lined with craftsman, Cape Cod, Tudor and colonial homes, and there are larger houses along the lakeshore.

Those older styles often mean finished basements, plaster or wood trim and hardwood floors, which matter for what can be dried and what is replaced. A wet hardwood floor in a Tudor, for instance, is a hardwood floor drying question first and a replacement question second.

Parks, roads and getting there

Bay Village has Cahoon Memorial Park, left to the city in 1917 by Ida Marie Cahoon along with 115 acres, and the Cleveland Metroparks Huntington Reservation, which includes the Lake Erie Nature and Science Center and BAYarts. Lake Road is a main road through the city, and the crew uses it to reach homes along the shore. Knowing the cross street or the nearest park helps us route correctly when you call.

Insurance and lake-driven water

Insurance for a lakefront home deserves attention. Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, but flood water from rising lake or ground water is usually excluded and needs separate flood coverage. Look up your address in the FEMA Flood Map Service Center and ask your agent. Scope and price are explained before any work starts.
