Sewage backup is a health hazard as well as a mess, because the water carries bacteria and viruses. If sewage is coming up a floor drain, a toilet or a tub in your Lakewood home, keep people and pets out, do not use water fixtures and call (216) 541-8541. The line is answered 24/7, and no one should wade through it without protection.

Industry practice calls this Category 3 water. It changes what is cleaned and what is thrown away. Clean water from a supply line or an appliance is handled differently, as covered on the water extraction page. Heavy rain flooding from outside is a flood damage case.

Sewage backup: what to do first

  • Stop using sinks, toilets, showers and washing machines. Every flush feeds the backup.
  • Keep children, pets and anyone with a weak immune system out of the area.
  • Do not try to clean it up with a household vacuum. Contaminated air from it can spread.
  • Turn off power only if you can do so from a dry spot. Otherwise call the utility.
  • Photograph the area from a safe distance and call the line.

Why not clean it yourself

Sewage water can contain pathogens, and fans spread them as aerosols. Gloves, boots and a respirator are the minimum for anyone in it. Health questions belong with a physician.

Is water coming up the drain or the toilet?

People rarely search for Category 3. They search for the symptom: a toilet that gurgles, a floor drain that bubbles, a basement that smells like a sewer. Those signs mean flow in the line is blocked or overloaded. A clogged private lateral, tree roots, a collapsed section and a heavy-rain overload of the public system can all push wastewater back up. The cleanup is called sewage backup cleanup, and it starts with identifying where the water is.

Lakewood sewer facts that matter

The City of Lakewood owns and operates its wastewater collection system, with about 166 miles of storm and sanitary sewer mains, and the owner is responsible for the pipe on private property. The city also says groundwater and stormwater entering aging pipes can use up capacity so that the sanitary system overloads and causes basement flooding. If neighbors have the same problem, tell the city, because a main issue is the city's to fix.

A sewer odor in a basement can have a simpler cause. The city says it usually means a dry floor drain trap, and that adding a small bucket of water to each floor drain about once a week prevents it, especially in winter. A plumber is the next call if the smell continues.

How sewage cleanup works

Crews wear protective gear and remove contaminated water with extraction equipment. Porous materials that soaked up sewage, including carpet, pad, insulation and drywall, are removed and bagged for disposal. Hard surfaces are cleaned and then disinfected.

Air movers and dehumidifiers dry what remains, sometimes with air scrubbers to help with odor. The crew checks readings before closing walls. Because sewage damage is serious, ask about the steps used and request your documentation for the insurance claim.

Insurance and backups

Many standard homeowners policies exclude sewer and drain backup unless it is added by endorsement, so check your policy or ask your insurer about backup coverage. Keep photos, the plumber's findings and receipts. If the cause was the public main, tell the city's water and sewer division.

Questions about sewage backup cleanup in Lakewood

Is sewage backup dangerous?

It can be, because the water may carry harmful microbes. Keep out, wear protective gear if you must enter and call a professional.

Why does my basement drain smell like sewage?

A dry trap is a common cause. The city suggests a small bucket of water in each floor drain about once a week. If the smell stays, call a plumber.

Who fixes the sewer line?

Owners are responsible for the pipe on private property, and the city handles the public main. A plumber's camera inspection shows which side the problem is on.

Can carpet be cleaned after sewage?

Usually not. Carpet and pad that soaked it up are removed, while hard surfaces are cleaned and disinfected.

Do you handle sewage backup cleanup emergencies at night or on weekends in Lakewood?

Yes. Backups often come with storms, so the line at (216) 541-8541 is answered 24/7. Same-day response is offered when crews are available, and keeping out of the water and stopping fixture use is the most important thing you can do first.
