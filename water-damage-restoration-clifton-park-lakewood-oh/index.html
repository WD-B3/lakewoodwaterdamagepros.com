Clifton Park is the northwest corner of Lakewood. The Lakewood Historical Society describes it as an exclusive residential district developed from the late 1890s, with curving streets that differ from the grid found elsewhere in the city, and large houses and estates close to Lake Erie. The Encyclopedia of Cleveland History notes that Clifton Park is on the National Register of Historic Places.

Large, older houses have large, older roofs, ceilings and plumbing runs, and a leak can travel a long way before it shows. If water is moving through a Clifton Park home, call (216) 541-8541 and describe where it is coming from.

Where it is

According to the Wikipedia article on Lakewood, Clifton Park is bounded roughly by Sloane Avenue on the south, Webb Road on the east, the Rocky River on the west and Lake Erie on the north. We describe it approximately because we have not found an official boundary for it, and the map below is a guide, not a survey.

What the flood map shows for Clifton Park

FEMA's effective map shows most of the sampled points in Clifton Park in Zone X, minimal flood hazard, with coastal high hazard areas mapped along parts of the Lake Erie shore and mapped flood hazard along the Rocky River valley on the western side. The mapped areas cover only part of the neighborhood.

The map does not say that any house has flooded, and Zone X is not a guarantee against water damage. Status for a specific address is not verified here.

Source type: FEMA National Flood Hazard Layer, effective map data queried in November 2026 at sample points across the area. This is area-level screening and not a property-level determination.

What water damage looks like in a large older home

In a large house, a roof or flashing leak can run down inside walls and show up as a stain two floors below. Plaster ceilings can hold water and then drop. A moisture meter and a thermal camera help find the real extent. The ceiling water damage page and the wall repair page explain how wet walls and ceilings are opened, dried and repaired.

Large houses often have finished lower levels, laundry rooms and several bathrooms, which means several places where an appliance line or supply line can fail. The appliance leak page covers the usual causes.

Lake storms and heavy rain

On the evening of August 23, 2023, the National Weather Service reported about 3.5 inches of rain in a couple of hours in Lakewood, and flooded basements in multiple homes were reported across the city. The report does not name Clifton Park. A storm that drives rain off the lake can still push water past worn flashing, window frames and roof edges, and the storm water damage page explains the cleanup.

Stop the water

Close the main valve or the fixture valve if you can reach it safely, and catch drips with buckets.

Mind the power

Do not touch wet outlets or breakers while standing in water. Call the utility or 911 about sparks or a bulging, wet ceiling.

Protect belongings

Move art, furniture and papers out of the wet area when it is safe, and photograph everything first.

Do not open a heavy ceiling

A bulging ceiling can hold a lot of water. Keep clear and let a crew drain it.

Extraction, drying and contents

Crews remove standing water, measure moisture and place air movers and dehumidifiers to dry floors, walls and framing. Valuable furniture, rugs and artwork need to be moved and protected, which is often the first thing the owner of a larger home asks about. The extraction page explains the first steps and the structural drying page explains how drying is confirmed.

Insurance paperwork

Coverage depends on the cause and on your policy, and your insurer decides. A claim for a larger home may involve more rooms and more belongings, so photographs, a list of damaged items and a written scope of work are worth keeping together. Ask your insurer what it needs and by when. We cannot promise that a claim will be paid.

Water damage questions in Clifton Park

Is Clifton Park in a FEMA flood zone?

FEMA's effective map shows most sampled points in Zone X, with coastal and river flood hazard mapped along the edges. Status for a specific property is not verified here.

Why does a roof leak show up far from the roof?

Water follows framing and pipes. In a large house it can travel through walls and ceilings before it appears as a stain.

What about a ceiling that is bulging?

Keep clear. A bulging ceiling holds water and can fall. A crew can drain it safely.

Can valuables be protected during cleanup?

Yes. Move or cover them when it is safe, photograph them and tell the crew what matters most.

Will my insurance cover the damage?

Your insurer decides based on the cause and your policy. We can provide photos, moisture readings and a scope of work.
