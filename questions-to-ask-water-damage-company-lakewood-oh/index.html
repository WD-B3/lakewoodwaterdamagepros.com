Questions to Ask a Water Damage Company Before You Hire in Lakewood, OH

By the Lakewood Water Damage Pros team · Homeowner guide · 2026

☎ Call (216) 541-8541

You may only hire a restoration company once or twice in your life, and you are usually doing it at the worst moment. A few questions asked early tell you a lot about how the job will go. This guide gives questions to put to any company, including us, and explains what a good answer sounds like. Nobody can promise perfect outcomes, so look for clear explanations, not guarantees.

Ten questions worth asking

  1. Are you licensed and insured? Ask for the certificate of insurance.
  2. What standards do your crews follow? Look for references to ANSI/IICRC S500 for water damage and S520 for mold.
  3. Will you explain the scope of work and the price before work starts?
  4. What water category do you think this is, and why?
  5. How will you measure moisture and show me when the structure is dry?
  6. What equipment will you use and how many days do you expect it to run?
  7. What will you remove and what will you try to save?
  8. Will you provide photos and readings for my insurer?
  9. Do you work directly with insurers, and who is responsible for the bill?
  10. Who is on call after hours if something goes wrong?

Notice that none of these asks for a guarantee. A reliable company explains what it sees, what it plans and how it will know the job is done.

Be careful with these promises

  • A promised arrival time during a storm, since demand varies.
  • A guaranteed insurance payout.
  • A promise that mold will never come back.
  • Pressure to sign before the scope is explained.
  • A request for the full payment before work begins.

A company that responds quickly and says so honestly is better than one that overpromises. When many homes are affected after a big storm, even good crews are stretched.

Ask about documentation

A good job leaves a paper trail: photos at the start, daily moisture readings, equipment logs and a final report when the structure reaches dry. This matters for your insurance claim and for your own records. The drying side of the job is explained on the structural drying page, and you can see the same logic described for water extraction.

Ask who does the work

Some companies send their own crews, and others subcontract. Neither is automatically wrong, but you should know who will be in your home and who is responsible if something goes wrong. Ask whether the person you talk to will be the person on site.

How to compare two quotes

Put the quotes side by side and list what each includes. Does it cover extraction, drying equipment, monitoring, removal, cleaning and disposal? Is a number given for the days of equipment? A lower price that leaves out monitoring or disposal may cost more in the end.

Ask each company to explain the difference. A good answer names specific items, such as how many air movers they plan to use and why. If a company cannot explain its own scope, that tells you something.

Trust your read of the conversation

Pay attention to how the company talks to you. Do they listen to what happened, ask questions and explain what they plan to do? Do they tell you what they do not know yet? Calm, specific answers are a better sign than a fast sales pitch.

Lakewood note: ask about local rules and licensing

Licensing rules for water damage work and for mold work can differ by state and by trade. In Ohio, plumbing and electrical work involve their own licensed trades, so ask whether a company uses licensed trades where the repair needs them, and ask whether the state licenses mold work, instead of assuming.

For building permits in Lakewood, the City of Lakewood building department is the place to ask what a given repair needs. A company that says it knows the local requirements should be able to point you to them.

Source type: the City of Lakewood building department as the authority to ask, plus industry standards S500 and S520 as published standards.

Questions homeowners ask

Should I get more than one quote?

If time allows, yes, but emergency water cannot wait long. Compare scope, standards and documentation, not only price.

What is S500?

ANSI/IICRC S500 is a published standard for professional water damage restoration. Ask how a company applies it.

Is it a red flag if a company wants to start right away?

No, speed is good in water damage. The red flag is starting without explaining scope and price.

Should I pay upfront?

Be wary of requests for full payment before work. Ask how and when payment is expected and get it in writing.

Does a company need to be a specific brand to be good?

No. Look for clear scope, standards, documentation and insurance, not a logo.
