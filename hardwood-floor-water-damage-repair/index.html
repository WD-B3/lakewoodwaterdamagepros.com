Hardwood Floor Water Damage Repair in Lakewood, OH

Cupping, crowning and dark stains mean water got into the boards or under them. Here is what can be dried and what must be replaced.

Hardwood absorbs water from the bottom first, so a floor can look fine while the subfloor stays wet. The first sign is usually cupping, when board edges rise higher than the center, or gaps that open and close with the seasons. Reading the damage: cupping, crowning, buckling and stains

  • Cupping: edges rise and the middle dips. The board absorbed moisture from below.
  • Crowning: the center rises. It sometimes follows cupping after the floor has dried.
  • Buckling: boards lift off the subfloor. This is serious and often means replacement.
  • Black or gray stains: water got through the finish and into the wood, or mold is beginning.
  • Gaps and loose boards: the wood shrank as it dried unevenly.

Cupping that appears within days often eases if the floor and subfloor are dried well. A board that has stayed wet for weeks may be permanently warped. A moisture meter on both the boards and the subfloor tells which case you have.

Can a hardwood floor be dried in place

Often yes, if clean water is caught within a day or two. Crews use specialized drying mats and tools that pull moisture up through the boards, and dehumidifiers run to keep the air dry. The process is slow, and the floor is monitored with readings, because drying too fast can cause cracking.

Floors over a concrete slab or a basement ceiling dry differently than floors over a vented joist space. The method depends on what is below the boards.

When boards come up

Engineered and laminate floors often swell at the edges, and the core does not recover. Floating floors lift in sections so the subfloor can dry underneath. Solid wood with finish damage is usually sanded after it is fully dry, not before.

Sanding wet wood seals moisture in and fails later. If a few boards are beyond saving, they can be replaced and blended in, though matching an old finish takes care.

Hardwood in Lakewood homes

Hardwood floors are a common feature of Lakewood's older houses and doubles, often over plank subfloors that can hold moisture against the underside. Many of these houses have basements below, and water from a basement leak or high humidity can raise the moisture in the joists and the boards. A moisture reading above and below the floor tells whether the source is above or below.

What to do while you wait

  • Lift rugs and furniture off the wet boards and wipe standing water.
  • Do not run a heat gun or a space heater on the floor, because fast drying can crack boards.
  • Photograph cupping or staining with the date.
  • Floor drying is measured against the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard.

Questions about hardwood floor water damage in Lakewood

Why is my hardwood floor cupping?

Moisture is reaching the bottom of the boards faster than the top. Leaks, damp basements and high humidity are common causes.

Should I sand a cupped floor right away?

No. Sanding before the wood has fully dried can lock in moisture and cause problems later. Dry first and measure.

Can laminate be saved after a leak?

Usually not once the edges have swollen. Laminate cores swell permanently, and the planks are typically replaced.

How long does drying a hardwood floor take?

It depends on the wood, the moisture and the conditions. It commonly takes days and is confirmed by readings, not by looking at the floor.
