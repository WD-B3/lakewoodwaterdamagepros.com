Serving Lakewood and nearby towns

The Gold Coast is the northeast end of Lakewood along Lake Erie, a strip of apartment buildings and condominium towers with a smaller number of houses. Street names in the area include Lake Avenue, Edgewater Drive, Cove Avenue and West 117th Street. A water problem in a large building behaves differently from one in a house, and this page is written around that difference.

If water is coming through a ceiling, a wall or a floor in a Gold Coast unit, call (216) 541-8541. The page explains what to do first, who to tell and how the drying works.

About the area

The Encyclopedia of Cleveland History describes the lakefront along Lake Road as the place where large apartment complexes were built in the 1950s and 1960s to meet housing demand. Survey estimates reported by a public statistics site put the area's population at under a thousand people in a little over four tenths of a square mile, with a mix of building ages and many rental units.

Because the area is dense and vertical, a single failure can affect several households. That is the real reason water damage here often involves more than one unit, and it does not depend on any claim about flooding.

What the flood map shows for the Gold Coast

FEMA's effective map shows the centre of the Gold Coast in Zone X, but it also shows coastal high hazard areas, mapped as VE coastal floodplain, along parts of the Lake Erie shoreline. That means the lakefront edge and the area behind it are not the same on the map.

A mapped coastal zone refers to storm surge and wave action from the lake. It is not a record that any building has flooded. Status for a specific property is not verified here.

Source type: FEMA National Flood Hazard Layer, effective map data queried in November 2026 at sample points across the area. This is area-level screening and not a property-level determination.

Leaks in multi-unit buildings

In an apartment or condo, the first call is usually to the building manager or the association, because shared plumbing, risers and roofs may be theirs to repair. Tell them quickly, and tell neighbors below you if the water is moving down. Water from one unit can spread across ceilings, walls and hallways.

Ceilings are often the first place the damage shows. A sagging or bubbling ceiling can hold a lot of trapped water, so keep out from under it and do not poke it. The ceiling water damage page explains how it is opened, dried and repaired.

Tell the building

Report the leak to the manager or association and, if it is yours, close the valve at the fixture.

Keep clear of ceilings

Do not stand under a sagging or dripping ceiling. Move people and valuables away.

Mind the electrics

Do not touch outlets, lights or breakers if they are wet or near the water. Call the utility or 911 about sparks.

Photograph and list

Photograph the damage and list affected belongings before moving anything, when it is safe.

Storms on the lakeshore

On the evening of August 23, 2023, the National Weather Service reported about 3.5 inches of rain in a couple of hours in Lakewood, and flooded basements in multiple homes were reported across the city. That was a citywide storm and the report does not name the Gold Coast. Lakefront buildings can still take wind-driven rain through windows and roof edges in a heavy storm, which the storm water damage page covers.

How drying works in a building

Crews measure the wet areas with moisture meters and a thermal camera, remove standing water, and place air movers and dehumidifiers to dry floors and walls. In a building with shared walls, drying may involve more than one unit, so access and coordination with the manager matter. The extraction page and the structural drying page explain the steps.

Insurance and the building's policy

In a condo or apartment, the building's policy and your own policy usually cover different things. Ask the association what it covers and ask your insurer what yours covers. Your insurer decides, and photos, readings and a written scope of work can help describe the loss. We cannot promise coverage.

Water damage questions in Gold Coast

Is the Gold Coast in a FEMA flood zone?

FEMA's effective map shows the centre of the area in Zone X and parts of the lakeshore in a coastal high hazard zone. Status for a specific property is not verified here.

Who do I call first about a leak in my apartment?

Tell the building manager or association, and close the valve at the fixture if it is yours and you can reach it safely. Then call for help with the cleanup.

The leak is in the unit above me. What should I do?

Report it to the manager, photograph the damage, keep out from under a sagging ceiling and call for extraction and drying.

Does the building's insurance cover my belongings?

Usually not. Ask the association what it covers and ask your own insurer about contents. Your insurer makes the decision.

Can drying be done without opening the ceiling?

It depends on how wet it is. A moisture meter and thermal camera show what is wet, and a crew explains what needs to be opened.
